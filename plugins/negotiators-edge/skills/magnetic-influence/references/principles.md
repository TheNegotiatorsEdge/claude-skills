# MAGNETIC Influence — Principles Reference

The eight principles from Tim Castle's *Magnetic Influence*. Read the relevant section when you
need to define a principle, justify a score, or give deeper coaching. Contents:

1. Principle definitions
2. Strong vs. weak examples
3. How each principle applies across business, communication, negotiation, and decision-making

## 1. Principle definitions

### M — Meaningful Impact
Becoming multidimensional — having more to you than work. It is about values, what you stand
for, and servant leadership; giving back and leaving a legacy. It is who you are at your core,
what you want to be known for, and how you operate, mixing integrity, passion, and commitment to
guide decisions. When people meet someone operating from meaningful impact, they already treat
them differently, because reputation, accomplishments, and purpose are transparent and honored.
The payoff: bigger rooms, a seat at the table, and calm while others rush around playing
someone else's game. Watch actions, not words, to tell who genuinely operates this way.

### A — Authenticity
As synthetic content floods the world, verified human voices become the scarce, valuable thing.
Authenticity is how you earn trust and cut through noise, and it is a license to get more
creative — once you're fully yourself, you negotiate from imagination and possibility rather
than fear. Take high-value, hard-to-scale actions (handwritten notes, curated dinners). In
deals, fuse authenticity with creative deal design: AI can generate many creative structures
(bundles, licensing, risk-sharing, value tiers), but it's a thought-starter, not a replacement —
you add the flair that reflects your true values and expands the pie.

### G — Gravitas, Generosity, Gratitude (G³)
**Gravitas** is calm confidence; people with it pull others into their orbit — they attract
rather than chase, focus intensely on a mission, and stay composed under pressure. It attracts
talent, investment, the right people, and opportunity. **Generosity** is a differentiator: give
unexpectedly and consistently, woven into how you do business — but balance it with boundaries to
avoid "generosity burnout." Be strategic: point people to resources rather than handing them the
full platter, which weeds out takers and protects your social capital and your network's time.
**Gratitude** removes fear and primes situations to go your way; it builds on generosity and
helps you forgive, let go, and see beyond the moment. Note the nuance in negotiation: in purely
distributive, one-off deals, over-thanking can read as weakness and be exploited; in cooperative
contexts, gratitude enhances and broadens outcomes. The trifecta keeps you warm, grateful, and
partnership-oriented.

### N — Networking Super Connector
Magnetic people have a prolific, elite network built over time, focused on adding value to
others — strengthening connections between people in their network, not just their own. They act
fast on sparks of opportunity, make introductions, and give freely. The network is one of
alignment, easy to commit to because it reflects who you are. You become the nexus where
conversations, ideas, and opportunities flow through you — you don't hoard them, you disperse
them to find their best purpose. Increase influence by offering value: invite people to events
they'd care about, share things with a personal note, promote others' work to amplify rather
than to get. Network is earned by helping people with no expectation of return.

### E — Energetic Vibration
There's an energy to winning, and you're always influencing through it. Successful negotiators
have high energy sensitivity — they read what people feel but aren't saying, and stay aware of
how they make others feel. This isn't about being loud or extroverted; it's about feeling and
dominant state. People constantly communicate feeling through body language and posture; learn to
read it, and learn to manage your own state. Feelings shape mindset, mindset shapes thoughts,
thoughts shape actions, actions shape results — and what you attract. In the age of AI, the human
element matters more: only you can reassure, connect, and build psychological safety, which lets
you influence without triggering resistance.

### T — Trust
Listening well — beyond the words, to intent, emotion, and fear — is the bridge to trust. People
buy from people with compelling stories; your reputation is your brand and it carries you further
than hustle alone. Once trust is destroyed it's very hard to rebuild. The fastest way to build it:
do what you say you'll do. Prepare for conversations so you walk in as the expert and consultant,
not a vendor — lead with curiosity, educate the client, and have a different kind of conversation
than the cookie-cutter pitch. Quality product and good service are table stakes; transparency and
humanity are where you actually build trust. See also the **TRUST AI™** framework in
`prompts.md`.

### I — Intuition
Intuition helps you stand out in a world of scripted mediocrity; AI should fuel creativity, not
replace it. Meditation strengthens the brain's decision-making and emotional-regulation circuitry
and helps you sense truth faster — ask "what's true here?" Unease usually signals a boundary
violation; calm certainty signals truth. Your body is always telling you something: when to
pause, when to speak, when to push. Intuition is strategic, not soft — it flags when someone is
holding back or when urgency is real, catches micro-expressions the conscious eye misses, and
delivers ideas right when you need them. Lean into it and it grows.

### C — Connection
Connection stems from genuine curiosity — a real desire to understand people's deepest wants and
needs (model: Da Vinci, who never stopped asking "why"). Your ability to connect comes down to
asking great questions, staying anti-insecure, listening more than you talk, and celebrating
others' wins. Curiosity signals genuine interest, builds rapport and trust, makes people feel
seen, and creates **psychological safety** — which lets people speak up, admit mistakes, and open
up without fear of judgment. It helps you avoid snap judgments, demonstrate empathy, and move from
small talk to real connection. Establishing psychological safety should be the goal.

## 2. Strong vs. weak examples

| Principle | Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Meaningful Impact** | No purpose, lost, works only because they "have to," not multidimensional, fills free time numbing/distracted, no deeper mission, lacks drive. | Multidimensional life guided by values; disciplined focus that rises above office politics; high self-esteem, not swayed by peer pressure; a deeper purpose shaping how they operate everywhere. |
| **Authenticity** | Wears a mask, one person outside and another inside, chases perfection, hides or lies to cover mistakes, struggles to connect. | Shows the chinks in the armor, which makes them real, reliable, relatable; people know where they stand; non-perfect side reads as genuine. |
| **G³** | Fakes gravitas by being loud and interrupting (revealing insecurity), keeps score on generosity, can't access gratitude, focused only on winning bigger for self. | Genuinely generous, makes others feel important, leads with big-heartedness, present, flows with gratitude while aiming high; uplifting presence; contagious optimism; a magnet for opportunity. |
| **Networking Super Connector** | A few weak ties, doesn't ask interesting questions, focused on what they can extract. | Constantly connects others and makes helpful introductions, remembers small details, strong cross-industry network, seen as valuable to know; people keep introducing others to them. |
| **Energetic Vibration** | Low vibration, bad thoughts about own capability, stuck and fed up, feels the world is against them, low empathy. | In alignment, spots opportunity everywhere, inspires with positivity and vision, lifts others, feels alive, radiates energy, takes on hard tasks, empathic. |
| **Trust** | "What's in it for me," takes shortcuts, unpredictable, hidden agenda, hides true intentions, false charm and empty compliments. | Seeks long-term partnerships, has integrity and values, adds value, shares transparently, consistent, does what they say — relied upon. |
| **Intuition** | Unsure, distrusts instinct, seeks validation, changes opinion easily even against gut. | Trusts their gut on whether to do a deal, values and strengthens intuition daily. |
| **Connection** | Searching for meaning, no close relationships, would do poor deals to get ahead, places little value on relationships, out for self only. | In alignment, servant-leadership mindset, improves own and others' lives by creating value, wide network of close connections, masterful connector, accountable, valuable to know. |

## 3. Application across contexts

| Principle | Business | Communication | Negotiation | Decision-making |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Meaningful Impact** | Build career/business around values, purpose, long-term performance. | Speak from depth and conviction, not image management. | Negotiate from self-worth, not insecurity; don't chase approval or accept weak deals. | Choose by principles and identity, not peer pressure or ego. |
| **Authenticity** | Build a brand people believe in because it's genuine. | Be open and relatable; people know where they stand. | Lower resistance — the other side trusts your intent. | Decide cleanly, without distorting reality to protect appearances. |
| **G³** | Lead with presence, optimism, contribution. | Make others feel important and heard. | Gravitas keeps you composed, generosity builds goodwill, gratitude prevents scarcity thinking. | Choose from abundance and confidence, not fear or scorekeeping. |
| **Networking Super Connector** | Build a strong ecosystem; be known as valuable to know. | Ask better questions, remember details, connect people. | A strong network is leverage and optionality before you start. | Decide with broader perspective and better information. |
| **Energetic Vibration** | Spot openings, create momentum. | Positivity and empathy make others receptive. | High-energy calm handles pressure and influences without forcing. | Recognize opportunity when aligned and present. |
| **Trust** | Built on consistency, transparency, partnership thinking. | Say what you mean, do what you say, no hidden agenda. | The base layer of influence; people deal better with the dependable. | Favor long-term value over shortcuts. |
| **Intuition** | Know when something is right, wrong, or premature. | Self-trust makes communication clearer. | Sense risk, timing, truth, and whether to do the deal at all. | Combine evidence with instinct rather than outsourcing judgment. |
| **Connection** | Build through service and real relationships, not extraction. | Communicate from respect, care, responsibility. | Create durable partnerships, not one-off wins. | Account for human impact and long-term mutual value. |

## 4. Full 1–5 scoring rubric

Anchors at 1, 3, and 5; interpolate 2 and 4.

**Meaningful Impact** — 1: Approval-seeking, ego-led, purely short-term. 3: Has a goal but not
anchored in values or mission. 5: Clear purpose, strong standards, long-term orientation,
grounded self-worth.

**Authenticity** — 1: Masked, evasive, over-managed, hiding truth. 3: Mostly clear but guarded or
polished. 5: Clear, real, congruent, approachable, honest about reality.

**G³** — 1: Loud, insecure, transactional, keeping score. 3: Respectful but ordinary presence.
5: Calm authority, generosity, gratitude, makes others feel seen.

**Networking Super Connector** — 1: Isolated, self-focused, narrow leverage. 3: Some useful
relationships, limited optionality. 5: Strong relationship capital, stakeholder awareness,
ecosystem thinking.

**Energetic Vibration** — 1: Negative, flat, defensive, contagious frustration. 3: Emotionally
neutral, steady enough. 5: Positive, empathic, constructive, opportunity-spotting under pressure.

**Trust** — 1: Hidden agenda, shortcuts, manipulative tone. 3: Reliable enough but not deeply
trusted. 5: Consistent, transparent, dependable, long-term partner mindset.

**Intuition** — 1: Distrusts instinct, over-defers, ignores red flags. 3: Some judgment but
hesitant. 5: Strong internal signal plus sound judgment on timing, risk, and fit.

**Connection** — 1: Extractive, detached, self-serving. 3: Polite but transactional. 5: Human,
respectful, accountable, mutual-value oriented.
